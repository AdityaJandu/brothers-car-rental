While driving at night offers cooler temperatures and less traffic, Indian highways after dark present unique and unpredictable challenges. Whether you're trying to reach your destination early or simply prefer night drives, these safety rules are non-negotiable.


## 1. The High Beam Menace

Oncoming vehicles frequently use high beams, blinding you temporarily. **Do not stare at the oncoming lights.** Instead, focus your vision on the left edge of the road or the white lane markings to maintain your lane discipline until the vehicle passes.


## 2. Beware of Unlit Hazards

Indian highways are notorious for unlit hazards. Watch out for:


- Trucks parked in the fast lane without hazard lights or reflectors.
- Stray cattle resting on the warm tarmac.
- Pedestrians and cyclists wearing dark clothing.

Reduce your speed by 20% compared to your daytime cruising speed to allow for a longer reaction time.


## 3. Use Your Lights Properly

Use your high beams only when the road ahead is empty. Dip them immediately when you see oncoming traffic or when following another vehicle closely. Ensure your headlights, taillights, and indicators are clean before setting out.


## 4. The Two-Second Rule (Extended)

During the day, a two-second following distance is recommended. At night, increase this to **four seconds**. Depth perception is significantly reduced in the dark, making it harder to judge the speed of the vehicle ahead.


## 5. Combat Driver Fatigue

The monotonous hum of the engine and the darkness can induce "highway hypnosis."


- Keep the cabin cool and well-ventilated.
- Play upbeat music or an engaging podcast.
- Take a 15-minute break every 2 hours to stretch and splash water on your face.


## 6. Windshield Visibility

A dirty windshield scatters oncoming light, creating massive glare. Clean your windshield (inside and out) before a night trip. Ensure your wiper fluid is topped up.


## 7. Avoid State Highways at Night

Stick to National Highways or Expressways (like the Yamuna Expressway or Delhi-Mumbai Expressway) at night. They are better paved, have clearer lane markings, and are generally safer. Rural state highways often lack streetlights, reflectors, and emergency assistance.
