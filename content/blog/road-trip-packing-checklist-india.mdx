The difference between a good road trip and a great one often comes down to preparation. Forgetting your phone charger, sunglasses, or cash at a toll can derail the fun quickly. Here's the **ultimate road trip packing checklist for India** — covering everything from essential documents to snacks, emergency gear, and tech.


## Category 1: Documents (Non-Negotiable)

- ☐ **Driving Licence** (original, not expired)
- ☐ **Rental Agreement** (digital copy on phone + printed backup)
- ☐ **Government Photo ID** (Aadhaar, passport, or voter ID)
- ☐ **Vehicle RC + Insurance** (kept in glove box of all Brothers vehicles)
- ☐ **Cash** (₹3,000-5,000 for emergencies — dhabas, rural fuel pumps, and parking may not accept UPI)
- ☐ **Hotel booking confirmations** (printed or offline-accessible)


## Category 2: Tech & Navigation

- ☐ **Phone car mount** — essential for hands-free navigation
- ☐ **Car charger** (dual USB with fast charging)
- ☐ **Power bank** (10,000+ mAh)
- ☐ **Offline Google Maps** — download before leaving (critical for mountain areas with no signal)
- ☐ **Music playlist** — pre-download on Spotify/YouTube Music for areas with no data
- ☐ **Aux cable or Bluetooth** — verify car audio connectivity before departing


## Category 3: Comfort Essentials

- ☐ **Sunglasses** — highway glare causes eye fatigue
- ☐ **Neck pillow** — for passengers napping on long stretches
- ☐ **Small cooler/insulated bag** — keep water and snacks cool
- ☐ **Wet wipes and tissues** — highway restrooms are unpredictable
- ☐ **Hand sanitizer** — dhaba meals require it
- ☐ **Plastic bags** — for car trash and wet items


## Category 4: Food & Drinks

- ☐ **2-3 litres of drinking water per person** — don't rely on roadside bottles exclusively
- ☐ **Mixed nuts and dry fruits** — high-energy, non-messy car snack
- ☐ **Biscuits/cookies** — universally loved car food
- ☐ **Mints/chewing gum** — helps drivers stay alert
- ☐ **Instant coffee sachets** — add to hot water at dhabas for a familiar brew


## Category 5: Emergency Kit

- ☐ **Basic first-aid kit** (bandages, antiseptic, pain relief)
- ☐ **Motion sickness medication** (essential for mountain passengers)
- ☐ **Torch/flashlight** — vital for nighttime tyre changes or mountain emergencies
- ☐ **Umbrella/rain jacket** — monsoon can strike without warning
- ☐ **Basic toolkit** — Brothers vehicles carry standard tools; verify at pickup
- ☐ **Emergency phone numbers** — 24/7 roadside assistance number saved in phone


## Category 6: Season-Specific Additions

### Summer (March-June)

- ☐ Extra water (dehydration risk)
- ☐ Sunscreen SPF 50+
- ☐ Hat/cap
- ☐ Electrolyte sachets (ORS)


### Monsoon (July-September)

- ☐ Rain jacket and waterproof bags
- ☐ Extra pair of dry clothes
- ☐ Waterproof phone pouch


### Winter (November-February)

- ☐ Thermal layers
- ☐ Warm jacket, gloves, woollen cap
- ☐ De-misting cloth for windshield
- ☐ Emergency blanket


## Frequently Asked Questions

### What's the single most important thing to pack?

Your driving licence. Without it, you cannot legally operate the rental car. Everything else can be purchased en route — but a driving licence cannot.


### Should I carry spare fuel?

On normal highway routes — no. Fuel stations are frequent. For remote mountain routes (Auli, Chopta, Spiti), having a 5-litre jerry can is prudent. Check with local authorities about carrying spare fuel — some regions restrict it.


### Is a car trash bag really necessary?

Yes. Keeping the car clean during the trip prevents cleaning charges at return. Also, littering on Indian highways and mountains is a legal offence in many states. Carry a bag, collect your trash, and dispose of it at your destination.


### Do I need cash or will UPI work everywhere?

UPI works at most fuel stations, toll plazas, and restaurants on major highways. However, small dhabas, village shops, rural parking lots, and some mountain area establishments still require cash. Carry ₹3,000-5,000 as backup.
