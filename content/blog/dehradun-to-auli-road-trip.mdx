## Why an SUV is Non-Negotiable for Auli

Let's be absolutely clear: a sedan or hatchback is not suitable for this drive. The route beyond Srinagar (Garhwal) involves:


- **Narrow mountain roads** with blind curves and steep drop-offs
- **Unpaved stretches** between Pipalkoti and Joshimath, especially after monsoon
- **Steep gradients** requiring strong engine torque and reliable brakes
- **Possible snow/ice** near Auli during winter months (December-March)

## Route Breakdown: Stage by Stage

### Stage 1: Dehradun → Rishikesh (43 km, ~1 hour)

A gentle warm-up drive on flat roads through the Doon valley. Fill your tank in Rishikesh — fuel stations become scarce after this point.


### Stage 2: Rishikesh → Devprayag (70 km, ~2 hours)

The mountain driving begins. The road follows the Ganga upstream through beautiful gorges. Devprayag — where the Alaknanda and Bhagirathi rivers merge — is a stunning photo stop. The river confluence is a visual spectacle of contrasting blue and green waters.


### Stage 3: Devprayag → Srinagar → Rudraprayag (80 km, ~2.5 hours)

Srinagar (Garhwal) is the largest town on the route and your best lunch stop. Rudraprayag marks another sacred river confluence. The road quality is generally good but watch for construction zones.


### Stage 4: Rudraprayag → Chamoli → Joshimath (85 km, ~3 hours)

The terrain gets seriously mountainous. The road narrows, gradients steepen, and hairpin bends multiply. Drive slowly, honk at every blind curve. Joshimath is the last major town — stock up on supplies.


### Stage 5: Joshimath → Auli (18 km, ~45 min)

The final ascent to Auli. The road is steep and narrow, but the views are extraordinary — Nanda Devi peak dominates the horizon. Alternatively, take the Joshimath-Auli ropeway (cable car) and leave your car parked in Joshimath.


## Best Time to Drive to Auli

- **Winter (December-March):** Skiing season. Roads may have snow patches near Auli. Chains may be required. Check road status daily.
- **Spring (April-May):** Snow melting, wildflowers blooming. Roads are generally clear. The best time for a driving holiday.
- **Summer (June):** Pleasant weather. Pre-monsoon clarity offers the best Himalayan views.
- **Monsoon (July-September):** AVOID. Landslides and road closures are common on the Rishikesh-Joshimath highway.
- **Autumn (October-November):** Post-monsoon clarity. Excellent driving conditions and crystal-clear mountain views.


## Essential Packing for the Auli Drive

- Warm clothing (layers — temperatures can drop to -5°C in winter)
- Offline maps downloaded (no network coverage between Chamoli and Auli)
- Basic first aid kit and emergency medicines
- Torch/flashlight (some mountain road sections are unlit)
- Extra drinking water and high-energy snacks
- Sunglasses (snow glare is blinding at altitude)


## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I drive to Auli in a sedan?

We strongly advise against it. The road beyond Srinagar has steep gradients, unpaved patches, and potential snow/ice near Auli. An SUV with high ground clearance and strong brakes is the only safe choice. [Browse our mountain-ready SUVs](/browse?category=suv "Book an SUV for Auli").


### Is the Rishikesh-Joshimath road safe?

Outside the monsoon season (July-September), the road is generally safe for experienced mountain drivers. Drive during daylight hours only. Start early from Dehradun (5-6 AM) to reach Auli before dark.


### Should I break the journey into 2 days?

Highly recommended. Stay overnight in Srinagar (Garhwal) or Rudraprayag, then complete the remaining drive to Auli the next morning. This avoids driving fatigued on dangerous mountain roads.


### Is there fuel available on the route?

Fuel stations exist at Rishikesh, Srinagar, Rudraprayag, and Chamoli. The gap between Chamoli and Joshimath has no fuel. Fill up completely at Chamoli.
