Often referred to as "Little Tibet," Spiti Valley is a cold desert mountain valley located high in the Himalayas. For self-drive enthusiasts, navigating the treacherous roads of the Spiti circuit is a rite of passage. Here's everything you need to know about undertaking this epic journey.


## The Two Routes: Shimla vs Manali

The Spiti circuit can be completed from two starting points. Both have their pros and cons.


### Route 1: Via Shimla (The Recommended Route)

**Path:** Delhi → Shimla → Narkanda → Kalpa → Kaza (Spiti) → Manali → Delhi


- **Pros:** Gradual acclimatization to high altitude (minimizes AMS). Open year-round (mostly) up to Kaza.
- **Cons:** Longer route. Takes 2-3 days just to reach Spiti.


### Route 2: Via Manali (The Adventurous Route)

**Path:** Delhi → Manali → Rohtang Pass → Kunzum Pass → Kaza


- **Pros:** Reaches Spiti much faster (in 2 days from Delhi). Highly scenic.
- **Cons:** Steep altitude gain increases risk of acute mountain sickness (AMS). Only open from June to mid-October.


## Choosing the Right Vehicle

Do not attempt the Spiti circuit in a hatchback or low-clearance sedan. The stretch between Losar and Gramphu (via Kunzum Pass) consists of unpaved, boulder-strewn water crossings (pagal nallahs). You need an SUV with:


- **High Ground Clearance:** Minimum 200mm.
- **Torque:** Diesel engines perform better on steep, oxygen-deprived inclines.
- **Reliability:** [A well-maintained 4x4 or robust RWD SUV](/browse?category=suv "Rent SUV for Spiti") is essential.


## Permits and Regulations

Indian citizens do not need an Inner Line Permit (ILP) to visit Spiti Valley, except for areas near the Tibetan border (like Shipki La, which isn't on the standard tourist circuit). However, if you enter via Manali, you need a Rohtang Pass permit, which can be applied for online.


## High-Altitude Driving Tips

1. **Water Crossings:** Cross water streams early in the morning before the glacial melt increases the water level. Always walk the crossing first to check the depth.
2. **Engine Braking:** Use lower gears while descending to avoid cooking your brakes.
3. **Fuel Up:** Fuel stations are scarce. Fill your tank at Kaza (the world's highest retail fuel station) and carry spare fuel in jerry cans if your vehicle's range is under 500km.
4. **Respect the Altitude:** Drink plenty of water. If you feel dizzy, do not drive. Stop and rest.
