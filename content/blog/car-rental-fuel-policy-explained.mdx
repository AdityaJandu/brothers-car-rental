The fuel policy is one of the most overlooked aspects of car rental — and one of the easiest places to lose money if you're not paying attention. Different rental companies use different fuel policies, and understanding them can save you ₹500-2,000 per trip. Here's the **complete guide to car rental fuel policies in India**.


## Types of Fuel Policies

### Full-to-Full (Best for You)

You receive the car with a full tank and must return it with a full tank. This is the fairest policy because you only pay for the fuel you actually use, at market pump rates. **Brothers Car Rental uses the Full-to-Full policy** across all vehicle categories.


### Same-to-Same

You receive the car with whatever fuel level it has (e.g., half tank) and return it at the same level. This can be tricky to match exactly and often leads to disputes.


### Full-to-Empty

You receive a full tank but don't need to refuel before returning. The catch? You've already paid for a full tank at the rental company's inflated rate (typically 20-40% above market prices) during checkout. You lose money on any unused fuel.


## How to Save Money on Fuel

- **Fill up just before returning:** Under Full-to-Full policy, refuel at the nearest petrol station before reaching the drop-off point. Some pumps near airports and city centres charge marginally more — use Google Maps to find the cheapest pump nearby.
- **Keep fuel receipts:** In case of any dispute about fuel level at return, your receipts prove you filled up recently.
- **Avoid the rental company's refueling service:** If you return the car without a full tank, most companies charge a refueling fee at 1.5-2x market petrol prices. This is legal but expensive. Always fill up yourself.
- **Track consumption:** Most modern cars display real-time fuel consumption. Monitor it to predict when you'll need to stop for fuel, especially on routes with sparse fuel stations (hill areas, desert stretches).


## Fuel Stations on Major Routes

- **NH48 (Delhi-Jaipur):** Fuel stations every 30-40 km. No shortage.
- **Yamuna Expressway:** Fuel stations at designated rest areas every 50 km.
- **Mountain roads (Uttarakhand):** Fill up in Dehradun, Rishikesh, or Srinagar. Stations become scarce beyond these towns.
- **Desert routes (Rajasthan):** Fill up in major towns. Gaps of 60-80 km between stations are common in the Thar desert.


## Frequently Asked Questions

### What happens if I return the car without a full tank?

A refueling charge is deducted from your security deposit. The charge is based on the estimated litres needed to reach full, multiplied by a per-litre rate (which may include a service fee). It's always cheaper to fill up yourself.


### Do I pay for fuel during the rental?

Yes. Fuel is the renter's responsibility throughout the rental period. You fill up at any petrol station using your own payment method. This is the case with all major rental companies in India.


### What fuel type should I use?

Use the fuel type specified on the car's fuel cap and in the rental documents (Petrol or Diesel). Using the wrong fuel will cause engine damage and you'll be liable for repair costs. When in doubt, check the booking confirmation which clearly states the fuel type.


### Is there a fuel surcharge on the rental?

No. Brothers Car Rental does not add fuel surcharges to the rental rate. You pay only for the fuel you consume at market pump prices.


### Can I use premium/high-octane fuel in a rental car?

Yes. You can use premium petrol (XP95, Speed, Power) without any issue. However, standard fuel is perfectly adequate for all rental vehicles. The marginal performance difference doesn't justify the 10-15% price premium for typical driving.
