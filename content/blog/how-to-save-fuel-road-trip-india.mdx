Fuel is often the second biggest expense on a road trip (after the rental itself). The difference between an aggressive driver and an efficient one can be 25-30% in fuel consumption — on a 1,000 km trip, that translates to ₹1,500-2,500 in savings. Here are proven **fuel-saving techniques for Indian road trips**.


## Driving Techniques That Save Fuel

### 1. Maintain Consistent Speed (80-100 km/h)

The sweet spot for most cars is 80-100 km/h on highways. Above 100 km/h, aerodynamic drag increases exponentially — driving at 120 km/h uses 20-25% more fuel than 90 km/h. Use cruise control if available.


### 2. Anticipate Traffic Flow

Look 200-300 metres ahead. If you see brake lights, traffic signals, or toll plazas approaching, lift off the accelerator early and coast. Gradual deceleration uses no fuel (engine braking). Slamming brakes at the last moment wastes all the fuel energy you spent accelerating.


### 3. Avoid Rapid Acceleration

Gentle acceleration from stops saves 10-15% fuel compared to flooring the pedal. Reach your cruising speed gradually over 15-20 seconds. The engine works most efficiently at moderate RPMs (2,000-2,500 for petrol, 1,500-2,000 for diesel).


### 4. Shift to Higher Gears Early (Manual)

For manual transmission cars, shift up as early as possible without the engine lugging. Higher gears = lower RPMs = less fuel consumption. The goal is to maintain 2,000-2,500 RPM at cruising speed.


### 5. Minimize AC Usage (When Practical)

Air conditioning increases fuel consumption by 10-15%. On cool morning drives or in hill stations, turn off the AC and open windows. However, at highway speeds above 80 km/h, open windows create more aerodynamic drag than AC — so use AC on fast stretches.


## Before Your Trip

### 6. Check Tyre Pressure

Under-inflated tyres increase fuel consumption by 2-3% per tyre. Check and inflate to the recommended pressure (found on the driver's door sticker) before starting your trip. All Brothers vehicles have tyres inflated to the correct pressure at pickup.


### 7. Remove Unnecessary Weight

Every 50 kg of unnecessary weight increases fuel consumption by about 1%. Remove items you won't need from the boot. That 10 kg gym bag, extra pair of shoes, and random books add up.


### 8. Plan Your Route for Fuel

The shortest route isn't always the most fuel-efficient. A slightly longer highway route with steady speeds can use less fuel than a shorter route through congested cities with constant stop-start driving.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does idling waste fuel?

Yes. An idling engine burns 0.5-1 litre per hour. If you're stopped for more than 60 seconds (at railway crossings, parking waits), turn off the engine. Modern cars with start-stop systems do this automatically.


### Is diesel really more fuel-efficient?

Diesel engines deliver 25-40% better mileage than petrol equivalents. On long highway trips, the fuel savings can be substantial. [Read our fuel type comparison](/blog/diesel-vs-petrol-rental-mileage "Diesel vs petrol comparison").


### Does driving in the morning save fuel?

Marginally — cooler morning temperatures improve engine efficiency slightly, and lighter traffic means fewer stops. But the primary advantage of morning driving is speed, not fuel savings.


### Should I fill up at highway pumps or city pumps?

Petrol prices vary by state. Fill up in Delhi/Haryana where prices are typically lower before entering Rajasthan or Uttarakhand where state taxes push prices higher.


### Does the type of petrol (regular vs premium) affect mileage?

For standard rental cars, premium petrol (XP95, Speed) offers negligible mileage improvement over regular petrol. Save your money — regular fuel is perfectly adequate and more cost-effective.
